## Note-Taking Apps: Free vs Paid

Students often rely on note-taking apps for lectures, research, and brainstorming. Two popular options are **Notion** and **Evernote**.

### Notion

- **Free plan**: Includes unlimited pages and blocks, but has a 5 MB file upload limit per file. It's great for basic note-taking and simple databases.
- **Paid plan**: The Personal Pro plan costs $5 per month (billed annually) and offers unlimited file uploads, version history (30 days), and more collaboration features. For students who store many attachments, the free limit can be a dealbreaker.

### Evernote

- **Free plan**: Allows 50 notes per month and a 60 MB upload limit per note. This is enough for light note-taking but not for heavy usage.
- **Paid plan**: The Premium plan at $7.99 per month gives unlimited notes, 10 GB monthly uploads, and offline access. If you scan a lot of lecture slides or PDFs, the free version will quickly hit its ceiling.

**Student scenario**: For online classes, you might take notes on a laptop and also snap photos of handwritten material. Notion's free tier handles text well but limits attachments; Evernote's free tier limits the number of notes. If you're a heavy note-taker, consider upgrading for storage or features.

## VPNs: Why You Might Need to Pay



VPNs are important for privacy, especially on public Wi-Fi at campus or coffee shops. However, free VPNs often come with trade-offs.

- **NordVPN**: The paid plan starts at around $3.99 per month (2-year plan) and includes unlimited data, fast speeds, and 6 device connections. While not free, it offers a 30-day money-back guarantee to try it risk-free.
- **ProtonVPN**: Has a free tier, but it only includes 3 server locations, no torrenting, and unlimited data? Actually, the free version provides unlimited data but limited to 3 locations and no P2P support. It's a good privacy-focused option for basic browsing.
- **Surfshark**: Paid plans start at about $2.49 per month (2-year plan), offering unlimited devices and strong security features. Its price is notably low for the feature set.

**Student scenario**: If you're abroad for a semester or just want to access university resources securely, a paid VPN like NordVPN or Surfshark may be worth the cost. But if you only need occasional privacy, ProtonVPN's free tier could suffice.

### Why a paid VPN might be worth it

- **Data limits**: Free VPNs often have data caps or slower speeds, which can make video calls and large downloads frustrating.
- **Security**: Paid VPNs typically offer stronger encryption and no-logs policies, but keep in mind that free ones may log or sell data.
- **Device coverage**: Many paid plans allow multiple simultaneous connections, useful for students with laptop, phone, and tablet.

## Cloud Storage: Free Space Limits Revealed

Cloud storage is essential for backing up assignments, syncing notes, and sharing project files. Let's compare the free and paid tiers of five popular providers: Google Drive, iCloud, OneDrive, pCloud, and MEGA.

| Provider | Free Storage | Paid Storage (Lowest Tier) | Price per Month |
|----------|--------------|-----------------------------|-----------------|
| Google Drive | 15 GB (shared with Google Photos & Gmail) | 100 GB | $1.99 |
| iCloud | 5 GB | 50 GB | $0.99 |
| OneDrive | 5 GB | 100 GB | $1.99 (standalone) or included in Microsoft 365 |
| pCloud | 10 GB | 500 GB (Premium) | $4.99 |
| MEGA | 20 GB | 400 GB | about $10.20 (Pro Lite) |

### Detailed Breakdown

- **Google Drive**: The free 15 GB is generous, but it's shared across your entire Google account. If you have a lot of photos in Google Photos, that space disappears quickly. Paying $1.99/month for 100 GB is cheap and often worth it.
- **iCloud**: Free 5 GB is quite limited for students who take many photos or videos. Upgrading to 50 GB is only $0.99/month, which is very affordable. It works seamlessly with Apple devices, but not as well with Windows or Android.
- **OneDrive**: Free 5 GB is small, but Microsoft 365 plans include 1 TB for $9.99/month (or $6.99/month for students in some countries). If you already use Office, the 1 TB storage makes it a great deal.
- **pCloud**: Free 10 GB is moderate, but the 500 GB plan at $4.99/month offers good value if you need more space and don't want to rely on a big brand.
- **MEGA**: Free 20 GB is generous, but paid plans are pricier. For students who need lots of storage without breaking the bank, MEGA's free tier might be enough.

**Student scenario**: If you're writing a thesis with plenty of PDFs, images, and video files, 15 GB can fill up fast. Google Drive's low-cost upgrade is a common choice. For collaboration in group projects, Google Drive and OneDrive offer strong sharing features.

## Office Suites: When Free Is Enough



Office suites are crucial for papers, presentations, and spreadsheets. Let's compare Microsoft Office and Google Workspace.

### Microsoft Office (Microsoft 365)

- **Free Web version**: You can use Word, Excel, and PowerPoint online with basic features and 5 GB OneDrive storage. It requires a Microsoft account and internet access.
- **Paid plan**: Microsoft 365 Personal costs $9.99/month (or $99.99/year) and includes desktop versions, 1 TB OneDrive, and advanced features like offline access and more. For students, Microsoft often offers a discount, but that's not always public.

### Google Workspace (formerly G Suite)

- **Free version**: Google Docs, Sheets, and Slides are free for personal use, with 15 GB storage. They are web-based, real-time collaboration, and have unlimited version history. Most students get this for free with a school email.
- **Paid plan**: Google Workspace Business Starter costs $6/user/month and adds custom email, admin controls, and more storage. For individual students, this is rarely necessary.

**Student scenario**: If you're typing a research paper, Google Docs offers seamless collaboration for group projects and automatic saving. However, if you need advanced formatting (like complex citation tools or offline desktop apps), Microsoft's paid version may be justified.

## Other Tools Worth a Look

Beyond the main categories, there are other tools that students might use.

### Project Management Tools

- **Trello**: Free version has unlimited boards, cards, and 10 MB file attachments per card. It's simple but lacks advanced features.
- **Asana**: Free version for up to 15 members, with basic features. Paid plans start at $10.99/user/month and add timeline view, workload, and more.
- **Monday.com**: Free version for up to 2 seats, with 3 boards. Paid plans start at $10/seat/month, but it's more expensive for larger teams.

For group projects, Trello's free tier is often enough to organize tasks. Asana and Monday.com free tiers are limited, but they might be more powerful if your team grows.

### Tablets

Some students find tablets helpful for note-taking and reading. A few budget options:

- **iPad (9th generation)**: Starts at $329 (base model). It's not free, but it's a popular choice with strong app support.
- **Samsung Galaxy Tab A8**: Cheaper, around $199, and comes with a 10.5-inch screen. It's good for basic tasks but less powerful.
- **Lenovo Tab**: Budget-friendly, but performance varies. For students who just need a note-taking device, these can be cost-effective.

### Domain Registration

If you're a student building a portfolio or a blog, domain registration matters. Here's a comparison of cheap registrars:

- **Namecheap**: .com domains cost about $11.98/year for the first year, renewal around $13.98. Often includes free WHOIS privacy.
- **Porkbun**: .com domains at $9.86/year, renewal $11.14. They offer free WHOIS privacy and a user-friendly interface.
- **Cloudflare Registrar**: .com domains at $8.19/year, renewal at cost (no markup). They don't upsell or offer free privacy, but they don't sell extras either.

For students, Cloudflare's at-cost pricing is attractive, but it's only for domain registration, not hosting. Porkbun is a good mix of price and simplicity.

### Screwdriver Sets (for DIY students)

If you're fixing your dorm furniture or building something, a basic toolset might help. For under $100, there are several jigsaw screwdriver sets, but we won't dive into details here.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are free versions of these tools really good enough for students?

Yes for many, depending on your usage. For note-taking, Notion's free plan with 5 MB upload limit is fine for text notes. For cloud storage, Google Drive's 15 GB might be enough for assignments but not for videos. For office suites, Google Docs is excellent for collaboration. However, if you need more storage or advanced features, paid plans can be worth it.

### What's the best way to save money on these tools?

- **Use free tiers first**: Try the free version for a month or two. If you hit a limit, it's time to upgrade.
- **Look for student discounts**: Microsoft, Adobe, and others offer educational pricing.
- **Share plans**: Some services allow family plans, but for students, it's often cheaper to buy individual plans annually.
- **Prioritize your needs**: Spend money where it matters most. Maybe a VPN is worth it for privacy, but a paid note-taking app is not.

### Is it worth paying for a VPN as a student?

If you frequently use public Wi-Fi or need to access geo-restricted content, a paid VPN offers peace of mind. However, if you only need occasional privacy, free ProtonVPN might be enough, though it has fewer servers and no P2P.

### Which cloud storage gives the most free space?

MEGA offers 20 GB free, which is generous compared to OneDrive's 5 GB or iCloud's 5 GB. But Google's 15 GB is better balanced if you already use Gmail and Google Photos.

### Are budget tablets good for students?

They can be for basic tasks like note-taking and reading. The Samsung Galaxy Tab A8 is affordable and capable for most school work, but for heavy multitasking, an iPad might be better.

## The Verdict: Which Upgrades Are Worth It?



Based on our comparison, here's where paying makes the most sense:

- **VPN**: Paid VPNs offer faster speeds, unlimited data, and more server locations, which is crucial if you're studying abroad or need reliable access.
- **Cloud Storage**: The first paid tier (100 GB) is cheap and often necessary for students with many files. Google Drive or iCloud are good picks depending on your device.
- **Office Suites**: For most students, free web-based Office or Google Workspace is enough. Only upgrade if you need desktop apps or offline access.
- **Note-Taking Apps**: Free versions are fine for most, but if you upload a lot of files, paying for Notion or Evernote can save you frustration.
- **Project Management**: Free Trello is great for simple tasks; paid Asana is worth it only for larger teams.

In short, evaluate your actual usage and don't overpay for features you'll never use. Start free, then upgrade only when you hit a wall. That's the smartest approach for any student budget.
